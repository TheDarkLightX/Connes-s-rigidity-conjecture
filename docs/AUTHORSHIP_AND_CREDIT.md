# Authorship, generation, credit, and responsibility

This document separates four concepts that are often collapsed: **scholarly authorship**, **legal copyright authorship**, **research contribution**, and **tool disclosure**.

This is a transparency policy for the repository, not legal advice.

## Why the AI is not placed in an author byline

Major scholarly-publishing policies do not treat large language models as authors. The common reason is accountability: an author must be able to approve the final work, answer for its accuracy and integrity, and take responsibility for corrections. An AI system cannot perform those human responsibilities.

Sources:

- Nature Portfolio, authorship: https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/authorship
- Nature Portfolio, AI policy example: https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/ai
- Elsevier, generative AI policy and authorship: https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/publishing-ethics-books/generative-ai-policies

Accordingly, the model is credited as a **generator/tool**, not as a legal or scholarly author.

## Is Dana Edwards the mathematical author?

There is no automatic rule that the owner of an AI account, the person who wrote prompts, or the person who supplied tools becomes the author of every generated mathematical step.

Scholarly authorship normally requires a substantial intellectual contribution **and** approval and accountability for the final work. Dana Edwards clearly contributed the research problem, project direction, custom tools, constraints, publication decision, repository stewardship, and repeated critical feedback. Those contributions should be credited precisely.

Because Dana Edwards states that he does not independently understand all of the mathematics deeply, the repository should not imply that he personally derived or independently verified every proof. Until a human is prepared to take full mathematical responsibility, the safest public description is:

- **project director**;
- **research sponsor and tool provider**;
- **repository owner and maintainer**;
- **responsible publisher of the website**;
- **not represented as the sole human derivation source for every proof**.

A later human expert who substantially checks, repairs, interprets, and accepts responsibility for a theorem may qualify for scholarly authorship under the policy of the relevant venue.

## U.S. copyright position

The U.S. Copyright Office's January 2025 report states that generative-AI output is copyrightable only where a human author determined sufficient expressive elements. Human-authored material perceptible in the output, or creative human selection, arrangement, and modification, may qualify. Mere prompting by itself is not sufficient.

Sources:

- U.S. Copyright Office announcement: https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html
- U.S. Copyright Office AI reports: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/

Consequences for this repository:

1. Purely machine-generated proof text may have no U.S. copyright protection by itself.
2. Human-written commentary, site design choices, selection and arrangement, original corrections, and other sufficiently creative human contributions may be protectable.
3. Copyright status and scholarly authorship are different questions.
4. The open-source license grants permissions to the extent rights exist; it does not manufacture copyright in unprotectable material.

## Required labels

Use these labels separately:

- **Generated** — produced by an AI model or automated program.
- **Finite-verified** — exhaustively checked only over a named finite domain.
- **Lean source present** — a formal file exists, regardless of build status.
- **Lean checked** — the theorem and complete dependency graph pass a clean standalone build.
- **Expert reviewed** — a named qualified human has reviewed the stated result and scope.
- **Published by Dana Edwards** — responsibility for making the repository/site public, not a claim that he manually derived every theorem.
