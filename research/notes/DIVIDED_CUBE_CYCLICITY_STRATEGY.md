# Divided-cube cyclicity: associated-graded proof strategy

Status: **conjectural proof plan**. Nothing in this note is promoted as a theorem until the stated obligations are proved in Lean or independently reviewed.

## 1. Setup

Let `F` be a field of characteristic three, let `R = F[t]`, and let

```text
V = R^3
```

regarded as an `F`-vector space with basis

```text
x(i,n) = t^n e_i,  i in {1,2,3}, n >= 0.
```

Let

```text
B = span_F { v ⊗ v ⊗ v | v in V } ⊆ V^(⊗3).
```

The Frobenius diagonal is the `F`-linear map

```text
d3 : B -> V,
d3(v ⊗ v ⊗ v) = v.
```

The proposed exact sequence is

```text
0 -> K -> B --d3-> V -> 0,
K = ker d3.
```

`d3` is equivariant for every `F`-linear automorphism of `V`.

## 2. A concrete basis for `B`

For basis vectors `x,y,z`, write

```text
[x,x,x] = x ⊗ x ⊗ x,
[x,x,y] = sum of the three tensors with two x slots and one y slot,
[x,y,z] = sum over the six permutations when x,y,z are distinct.
```

In characteristic three, polarization with the scalars `1` and `2` shows that every such multiset-symmetrized tensor lies in the span of pure cubes. Conversely every pure cube expands into these tensors. Therefore the expected basis of `B` is indexed by multisets of three basis vectors.

This predicts

```text
dim B_N = binomial(3N+2,3)
```

for `V_N = (F[t]/t^N)^3`, exactly as observed by the Julia oracle.

The map `d3` satisfies

```text
d3([x,x,x]) = x,
d3([x,x,y]) = 0,
d3([x,y,z]) = 0.
```

Hence `K` has the basis of all non-diagonal multisets.

### Lean obligations

1. Prove `B` equals the subspace of permutation-invariant cubic tensors, or prove directly that the displayed multiset family is a basis.
2. Prove the diagonal formula for `d3` on that basis.
3. Prove surjectivity and the exact kernel description.

## 3. Reduction of cyclicity of `B` to cyclicity of `K`

Let

```text
q = [e1,e1,e1]
and
w = [e1,e2,e3].
```

The elementary group `E3(R)` acts on `B`.

The orbit span of `q` maps under `d3` onto the orbit span of `e1` in `V`. Since elementary matrices generate every polynomial basis vector from `e1`, this image should be all of `V`.

The tensor `w` itself belongs to the orbit span of `q`: inclusion-exclusion applied to pure cubes of the unimodular vectors

```text
e1,
e2,
e3,
e1+e2,
e1+e3,
e2+e3,
e1+e2+e3
```

isolates the all-distinct orbit sum. Every listed vector is the first column of an elementary matrix over `F[t]`.

Consequently:

> If `K` is generated by `w` as an `F[E3(R)]`-module, then `B` is generated by `q`.

Proof reduction: for `b in B`, generate an element `c` in the orbit span of `q` with `d3(c)=d3(b)`. Then `b-c` lies in `K`, which is generated by `w`, and `w` already lies in the orbit span of `q`.

This reduction is preferable to proving pure-cube cyclicity monomial by monomial.

## 4. Constant-degree kernel and the seven-dimensional module

For the constant subspace `W=F^3`,

```text
dim B(W) = 10,
dim K(W) = 7.
```

A basis of `K(W)` is

```text
[e_i,e_i,e_j]  for i != j   (six vectors),
[e1,e2,e3]                    (one vector).
```

The weight of `[e_i,e_i,e_j]` under the diagonal torus of `SL3` is

```text
2 epsilon_i + epsilon_j = epsilon_i - epsilon_k
```

where `{i,j,k}={1,2,3}` and `epsilon_1+epsilon_2+epsilon_3=0` on `SL3`.

These are the six roots of type `A2`; the all-distinct vector has weight zero. This strongly suggests

```text
K(W) ≅ psl_3(F)
```

in characteristic three: `sl_3(F)` has a one-dimensional scalar center because `trace(I)=3=0`, and the quotient has dimension seven.

### Why this matters

If this identification is correct, the constant kernel is a known cyclic `SL3(F)`-module. The all-distinct vector should correspond to the remaining Cartan direction and generate all six root spaces under elementary unipotents.

### Falsifiers

- The proposed module map may fail to intertwine one elementary transvection.
- Over fields larger than `F3`, the constant module may have an unexpected proper submodule.
- `K(W)` may be a nontrivial extension with the same weights rather than `psl_3`.

### Lean portal

Define an explicit map on the seven displayed basis vectors to `sl_3(F) / F·I` and check equivariance only for elementary generators. Dimension plus injectivity then gives the equivalence.

## 5. Polynomial degrees as a filtration

Filter `V`, `B`, and `K` by maximum or total `t`-degree. For an elementary transvection

```text
u_ij(t^r) = I + t^r E_ij,
```

the difference `u_ij(t^r)-1` acts as a degree-raising operator.

The proposed induction invariant is a well-founded order on multiset basis elements combining:

1. total polynomial degree;
2. maximum exponent;
3. coordinate-label multiplicity pattern;
4. lexicographic exponent tuple.

Applying a transvection to an already generated tensor yields the desired leading multiset plus terms lower in this order. The lower terms are removed by induction.

This is the same structural move that succeeded in the infinite-orbit proof: compare a numerical support invariant instead of comparing full tensors.

## 6. Why the all-distinct generator is promising

A transvection that sends `e2` to `e2 + f e1` acts on the all-distinct vector by

```text
[e1,e2,e3] -> [e1,e2,e3] + [e1,f e1,e3].
```

There are no quadratic or cubic terms because the source contains only one `e2` slot. Thus one action directly creates a two-equal-coordinate kernel tensor with an arbitrary polynomial coefficient in one slot.

Constant elementary matrices permute and mix coordinate labels. Further polynomial transvections move polynomial exponents between slots. This gives a triangular generation scheme for every non-diagonal multiset basis vector.

### Core induction target

For every non-diagonal multiset of polynomial basis vectors

```text
M = [x(i,a), x(j,b), x(k,c)],
```

construct a finite linear combination of elementary translates of `w` equal to

```text
M + lower_terms(M).
```

The chosen order must make every `lower_terms(M)` strictly smaller.

## 7. Finite evidence

For `N=1,2,3,4`, the checked Julia oracle over `F3[t]/(t^N)` found:

| N | dim V | dim B | dim K | span of orbit of `q` | span of orbit of `w` | common fixed dimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 10 | 7 | 10 | 7 | 0 |
| 2 | 6 | 56 | 50 | 56 | 50 | 0 |
| 3 | 9 | 165 | 156 | 165 | 156 | 0 |
| 4 | 12 | 364 | 352 | 364 | 352 | 0 |

This evidence supports the proposed induction but cannot rule out a failure first appearing at a larger degree.

## 8. Relation to the ternary rigidity construction

A proof of kernel cyclicity would close more than finite generation:

- `B` would be finitely generated under the acting elementary group;
- `K=ker d3` would have a distinguished intrinsic generator orbit;
- the proposed torsion exact sequence could use a concrete kernel module rather than an opaque divided-power object;
- the existing infinite-orbit theorem would rule out hidden finite `K`-orbits;
- the property-(T) and ICC stages would receive a cleaner representation-theoretic input.

It does **not** by itself prove property (T), ICC, the `3^(3n)` intrinsic invariant, or common von Neumann algebras.
