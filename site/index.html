Problem status and priority

The reported constructions are prior art. This repository studies their algebraic frontier.

OpenAI's binary-carry construction and Shuoxing Zhou's independent action-shear construction report counterexamples to the broad ICC property-(T) formulation. Their measured-action mechanisms, common-factor conclusions, and binary finite-orbit strategies are not claimed as new here.

Possible new contributions are narrower: a rank-three tensor-orbit theorem, stable and critical divided-power cyclicity, multivariate extensions, and a prime-rank detector synthesis. Only the first item is currently represented by a general Lean theorem. The others remain proof candidates.

The strongest formal theorem

A symmetry assumption forces an infinite elementary orbit.

Assume: F is a field, w ≠ 0, and swapping the first two factors of w ∈ (F[t]^3)⊗³ leaves w fixed.

Then: there are distinct coordinates i,j and a starting degree N such that

{ uij(tN+n) · w : n ≥ 0 } is infinite.

The proof converts the tensor into polynomial coordinate blocks, finds a nonzero extremal block, and chooses a transvection whose observed degree grows without bound. A divided cube is fixed by the same swap, so the divided-cube orbit theorem follows as a corollary.

Scope check

What this theorem does not prove

  1. It does not prove cyclicity of the divided cube.
  2. It does not cover arbitrary rank and tensor degree.
  3. It does not construct property-(T) groups or identify group factors.
  4. It does not establish that the statement is new in the literature.
A checked correction

The cubic diagonal cannot be untwisted over every characteristic-three field.

Suppose an F-linear map satisfies d(x³)=x. Linearity gives d(x³)=x³d(1)=x³, while the retraction assumption gives d(x³)=x. Therefore x³=x for every scalar.

F-linear retraction ⇒ x³ = x for every x ∈ F

This theorem records negative knowledge. It rejects the earlier general-field formulation and isolates the need for a Frobenius-twisted codomain. It does not by itself construct that corrected map.

Written proof candidates

The promising mathematics begins where the formal graph stops.

01

Stable and critical cyclicity

For R=F_p[t₁,...,t_d] and V=R^r, the proposed theorem makes the Frobenius kernel and full divided pth power cyclic when r>p, and also when r=p for odd p.

02

General tensor orbits

The rank-three Lean proof suggests an arbitrary-rank theorem: nonzero tensors have infinite elementary orbit for tensor degree m<r, with a first-two-symmetric critical case at m=r.

03

Prime-uniform carry families

The algebra and detector calculations suggest minimal rank r_p=max(p,3) and a finite-orbit cardinal p^(r_p n). The construction still lacks checked group-theoretic and operator-algebraic interfaces.

Specialists must still check mathematical relevance, hidden modeling assumptions, literature priority, and the unformalized interfaces between fields.

Publication assessment

The repository supports a formalization note now, not yet a full research paper.

The checked rank-three orbit theorem, Frobenius correction, carry identities, and exact kernel cardinality are substantial enough for a technical exposition or formalization note. They are not yet enough for a strong paper claiming a new counterexample family.

A focused research paper becomes plausible if the stable and critical cyclicity theorem or the general tensor-orbit theorem survives specialist review, receives a complete proof, and is shown to be genuinely absent from the modular divided-power literature. Formalizing one of those general theorems would make the case much stronger.

Paper gate

Minimum evidence still needed

  1. A complete general proof, preferably machine checked.
  2. A specialist prior-art review in modular representation theory.
  3. Independent proof review.
  4. A sharply stated theorem that does not depend on unfinished operator-algebra bridges.
