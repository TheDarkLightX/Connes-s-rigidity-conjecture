Main structural statement

Coordinate multiplicities determine the spectral blocks

Let \(p\) be prime and put

\[ k=\mathbf F_p,\qquad V=k^r\otimes_k k[t],\qquad P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_p],\qquad S=P^{\mathfrak S_p}=k[e_1,\ldots,e_p]. \]

The group \(\mathfrak S_p\) simultaneously permutes tensor positions and spectral variables. For a partition \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_\ell)\vdash p\), let

\[ H_\lambda= \mathfrak S_{\lambda_1}\times\cdots\times \mathfrak S_{\lambda_\ell}. \]

If \(a_s(\lambda)\) counts parts of size \(s\), the number of coordinate multisets of type \(\lambda\) is

\[ m_{r,\lambda}= \frac{r!}{(r-\ell)!\prod_s a_s(\lambda)!}. \]

Spectral orbit-block decomposition

\[ \Gamma^p(V) \cong \bigoplus_{\substack{\lambda\vdash p\\\ell(\lambda)\le r}} \left(P^{H_\lambda}\right)^{\oplus m_{r,\lambda}} \]

as graded modules over \(S=P^{\mathfrak S_p}\). For one coordinate orbit, an invariant family is uniquely determined by its polynomial at a representative tuple; the stabilizer condition is exactly membership in \(P^{H_\lambda}\).

Each proper Young subgroup has order prime to \(p\). Its invariant block is therefore a projective, hence free, \(S\)-module. Its Hilbert numerator is the Gaussian multinomial

\[ C_\lambda(q)= \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-q^j)} {\prod_{a=1}^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{\lambda_a}(1-q^j)}. \]

The total numerator is

\[ B_{p,r}(q)= \sum_\lambda m_{r,\lambda}C_\lambda(q), \qquad B_{p,r}(1)=r^p. \]

The spectral diagonal lemma

For a symmetric polynomial \(f\in S\), let \(\Delta(f)\) retain only coefficients whose \(p\) spectral exponents are equal:

\[ \Delta(f)= \sum_{n\ge0} [t_1^n\cdots t_p^n]f\;\tau^n. \]

The coefficient of \(t_1^n\cdots t_p^n\) in \(e_jf\) is a sum of \(inom pj\) equal coefficients, because \(f\) is symmetric. Therefore

\[ \Delta(e_jf)=0\quad(1\le j

Since every intermediate binomial coefficient is divisible by \(p\), the diagonal extraction is exactly

\[ S\longrightarrow k[\tau],\qquad e_1,\ldots,e_{p-1}\longmapsto0, \quad e_p\longmapsto\tau. \]

Its kernel is the regular-sequence ideal

\[ I_p=(e_1,\ldots,e_{p-1}). \]

The kernel has one nonfree spectral sector

The Frobenius diagonal vanishes on every mixed-label coordinate block. On each of the \(r\) all-same-label blocks it is the quotient \(S\to S/I_p\). Hence

Frobenius-kernel decomposition

\[ K_{p,r}:=\ker d_p \cong I_p^{\oplus r} \oplus \bigoplus_{\lambda\ne(p)} \left(P^{H_\lambda}\right)^{\oplus m_{r,\lambda}}. \]

Its Hilbert numerator is therefore

\[ K_{p,r}(q)= B_{p,r}(q)-r\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-q^j). \]

All mixed-label sectors are free. The only nonfree part consists of \(r\) copies of \(I_p\), so the truncated Koszul complex gives the complete minimal resolution. In positive homological degree,

\[ F_j= \left[ \bigoplus_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\\|J|=j+1}} S\!\left(-\sum_{a\in J}a\right) \right]^{\oplus r}, \qquad1\le j\le p-2. \]

This yields the prime-uniform homological phase change

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} &\operatorname{pd}_S K_{p,r}&\operatorname{depth}_S K_{p,r}\\ \hline p=2&0&2\\ p\ge3&p-2&2. \end{array} \]

Up to its grading shift, the top Ext group is \((S/I_p)^{\oplus r}\). The kernel therefore remembers the Frobenius spectral curve homologically even though it has the same generic \(S\)-rank \(r^p\) as the whole divided power.

The ternary numerator is a Betti table

For \(p=r=3\), the coordinate types are

  • three \((3)\)-blocks with stabilizer \(\mathfrak S_3\);
  • six \((2,1)\)-blocks with stabilizer \(\mathfrak S_2\);
  • one all-distinct block with trivial stabilizer.

The free spectral decomposition is

\[ \Gamma^3(\mathbf F_3^3[t]) \cong S^{10}\oplus S(-1)^8\oplus S(-2)^8\oplus S(-3). \]

The Frobenius kernel has the proposed minimal presentation

\[ 0\longrightarrow S(-3)^3 \longrightarrow S^7\oplus S(-1)^{11}\oplus S(-2)^{11}\oplus S(-3) \longrightarrow K_{3,3}\longrightarrow0. \]

Consequently

\[ \operatorname{Hilb}(K_{3,3};q)= \frac{7+11q+11q^2-2q^3} {(1-q)(1-q^2)(1-q^3)}. \]

The negative coefficient is not a failed dimension count. The free generators contribute \(+q^3\); the three Frobenius-sector syzygies contribute \(-3q^3\). Their difference is the observed \(-2q^3\).

Binary rank three is spectrally free

\[ \Gamma^2(\mathbf F_2^3[t]) \cong S^6\oplus S(-1)^3, \qquad K_{2,3}\cong S^3\oplus S(-1)^6. \]

Here \(I_2=(e_1)\) is principal. The change from \(p=2\) to odd primes is therefore visible both in the cyclicity proof and in the spectral homological dimension.

Why this matters for the rigidity program

  1. It converts the earlier Hilbert-series observation into an exact module decomposition rather than a numerical guess.
  2. It rules out any proposed identification of the odd-prime kernel with a spectrally free global-Weyl-type module.
  3. It provides a smaller formalization target: mixed-label freeness, the diagonal coefficient lemma, and a standard Koszul resolution.
  4. It separates current-group cyclicity from spectral generation. A single group orbit may cross many spectral blocks.

Evidence and novelty boundary

The repository's new symbolic oracle checks partition multiplicities, Gaussian multinomials, spectral ranks, kernel numerators, and every Betti-polynomial alternating sum for the declared prime/rank cases. It reproduces the ternary and binary formulas exactly.

The ingredients—symmetric polynomial freeness, Young-subgroup invariants, Koszul complexes, Frobenius twists, and global Weyl modules—are classical. The possible new contribution is their exact assembly into this Frobenius-kernel resolution and its application to the carry construction. Specialist review and a targeted prior-art search remain necessary before a novelty claim.
