Main structural statement

Coordinate multiplicities determine the spectral blocks

Let

\[ k=\mathbf F_p,\quad V=k^r\otimes_k k[t],\quad P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_p],\quad S=P^{\mathfrak S_p}=k[e_1,\ldots,e_p]. \]

For a partition \(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_\ell)\vdash p\), put

\[ H_\lambda= \mathfrak S_{\lambda_1}\times\cdots\times \mathfrak S_{\lambda_\ell}. \]

If \(a_s(\lambda)\) counts parts of size \(s\), the number of coordinate multisets of this type is

\[ m_{r,\lambda}= \frac{r!}{(r-\ell)!\prod_s a_s(\lambda)!}. \]

Spectral orbit-block decomposition

\[ \Gamma^p(V) \cong \bigoplus_{\substack{\lambda\vdash p\\\ell(\lambda)\le r}} \left(P^{H_\lambda}\right)^{\oplus m_{r,\lambda}} \]

as graded modules over \(S\). An invariant family on one coordinate orbit is determined by its polynomial at a representative tuple; the stabilizer condition is exactly membership in \(P^{H_\lambda}\).

The Hilbert numerator of one block is

\[ C_\lambda(q)= \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-q^j)} {\prod_{a=1}^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{\lambda_a}(1-q^j)}. \]

Therefore

\[ B_{p,r}(q)=\sum_\lambda m_{r,\lambda}C_\lambda(q), \qquad B_{p,r}(1)=r^p. \]

The spectral diagonal lemma

For symmetric \(f\in S\), let \(\Delta(f)\) retain only coefficients whose spectral exponents are all equal:

\[ \Delta(f)= \sum_{n\ge0}[t_1^n\cdots t_p^n]f\;\tau^n. \]

In \(e_jf\), the required coefficient is a sum of \(inom pj\) equal coefficients. Hence in characteristic \(p\),

\[ \Delta(e_jf)=0\quad(1\le j

Thus diagonal extraction is exactly the quotient

\[ S\longrightarrow k[\tau],\qquad e_1,\ldots,e_{p-1}\longmapsto0, \quad e_p\longmapsto\tau, \]

with kernel

\[ I_p=(e_1,\ldots,e_{p-1}). \]

All nonfreeness lies in one regular-sequence ideal

The Frobenius diagonal vanishes on every mixed-label block. On each of the \(r\) all-same-label blocks it is \(S\to S/I_p\). Therefore

Frobenius-kernel decomposition

\[ K_{p,r}:=\ker d_p \cong I_p^{\oplus r} \oplus \bigoplus_{\lambda\ne(p)} \left(P^{H_\lambda}\right)^{\oplus m_{r,\lambda}}. \]

Its Hilbert numerator is

\[ K_{p,r}(q)= B_{p,r}(q)-r\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-q^j). \]

The ideal \(I_p\) has the truncated Koszul resolution. In positive homological degree,

\[ F_j= \left[ \bigoplus_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\\|J|=j+1}} S\!\left(-\sum_{a\in J}a\right) \right]^{\oplus r}, \qquad1\le j\le p-2. \]

The predicted homological phase change is

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} &\operatorname{pd}_S K_{p,r}&\operatorname{depth}_S K_{p,r}\\ \hline p=2&0&2\\ p\ge3&p-2&2. \end{array} \]

The ternary numerator is a Betti table

For \(p=r=3\), the free spectral module is

\[ \Gamma^3(\mathbf F_3^3[t]) \cong S^{10}\oplus S(-1)^8\oplus S(-2)^8\oplus S(-3). \]

The candidate minimal presentation is

\[ 0\longrightarrow S(-3)^3 \longrightarrow S^7\oplus S(-1)^{11}\oplus S(-2)^{11}\oplus S(-3) \longrightarrow K_{3,3}\longrightarrow0. \]

Consequently

\[ \operatorname{Hilb}(K_{3,3};q)= \frac{7+11q+11q^2-2q^3} {(1-q)(1-q^2)(1-q^3)}. \]

The degree-three free generator contributes \(+q^3\). The three Frobenius-sector syzygies contribute \(-3q^3\). This explains the previously mysterious coefficient \(-2q^3\).

Binary rank three is spectrally free

\[ \Gamma^2(\mathbf F_2^3[t]) \cong S^6\oplus S(-1)^3, \qquad K_{2,3}\cong S^3\oplus S(-1)^6. \]

Here \(I_2=(e_1)\) is principal. The transition from binary to odd primes is visible both in cyclicity and in spectral homological dimension.

Meaning and limits

  1. The old Hilbert series now has an exact module explanation.
  2. Odd-prime kernels are not spectrally free.
  3. The result offers smaller formalization targets: orbit blocks, the diagonal lemma, and a Koszul complex.
  4. Current-group cyclicity and spectral generation remain different notions.

Symmetric polynomial freeness, Young-subgroup invariants, Koszul complexes, Frobenius twists, and global Weyl modules are classical. The possible new content is their exact assembly into this kernel resolution and its use in the rigidity carry program. Specialist review and a targeted priority search remain necessary.
